Hello World!

I have a website. 

It really is funny if I look at it. Some of you might not know, but back in primary school, I had one dream and one dream only. To get into this one class in high school. It was the best in the county and it specialised in mathematics and informatics. 

All good, I worked very hard, and despite all efforts of my then ‘best friend’, who had other type of dreams and aspirations, I got in. Yaaay (congratulatory applauses to self!). It was supposed to be a dream, this class filled with like-minded peers, these deeply vocationally inclined teachers who will guide my way much like a light in the dark and me, this young very determined soon-to-be scientist. But of course, like in any ordinary tale, things don’t go the way you imagine. 

The class? Filled with people who already had spent their primary years at that very high school, who, for a reason unknown to me, thought they had some extraordinary rite of passage. The way they themselves but also, as I would soon come to find out, all the teachers that taught the younger classes too, would position these classmates, was as if God/Buddha/The Universe (or whichever higher power may be recognised) him/her/themselves personally came to them one by one and hand-picked them, much like the Pope is elected (I think – don’t hold me to it – religion is not my thing). It was them and the others, like a top-secret society that one cannot dare to dream to enter, organised naturally like a cult, where one was thought to be the unruly leader and the rest the unwilling followers. The story about this self-proclaimed leader I will keep for another time. 

Amongst the uninitiated ones, apart from me and my dear old friend from Primary, stood a multitude of diverse characters: some were funny, some were quirky (definitely me included), some were deeply misunderstood. We were all very different from one another. It was very difficult to find things in common, especially since from the beginning it was very obvious that we were supposed to hate each other, in these hunger games of proving to the teachers which one of us was ‘better’. Looking back, all through those four years, we were successfully fulfilling their agenda of terror, never truly being ‘united’ in anything. A pack of lonely wolves, who were put together only to survive, because we all knew the winter was coming.

The teachers? Oh the high hopes I had and oh the high disappointment I suffered. Most of them remnants of the communist regime, deeply convinced they needed to keep the same ethics, and, in my humble opinion, also deeply convinced they needed to torture us by whichever means necessary. The favouritism was not masked, neither was the looking down on the ‘outsiders’. Their instrument of torture? Low marks awarded unfairly in the hopes your parents would be desperate enough to think what on earth had happened with their high-achieving child, who all of a sudden was the bottom of the food chain, to eventually book private lessons with the very teachers in question. A system that had been working so well for far too long to be disturbed. 

Why have I taken you, dear readers, down this painful memory lane? Well to give you the context you need to fully understand why building this website was not on the cards for me. 

The Informatics class, the one and only, the long-awaited informatics class. I was so so deeply excited to be part of the next generation of scientists. I knew I wanted to learn how to code. I also knew that my destiny lived outside the software engineering realm, aspiring to do research and dreaming to change the world! However, still very excited about this new opportunity that was rising, which was, of course, a direct result of all my hard work. And it was going to be delivered by a woman! How wonderfully moving! A woman, a coder, in a field full of men! I was going to be part of history, of course, I was probably going to follow her lead and as I was imagining myself holding my first prestigious award in my hands, I would, without doubt, thank her, my first scientific role model, the woman who opened my heart and my mind to the world of coding. 

But of course, I wouldn’t tell you this story if it didn’t have some sort of tragic plot twist. This amazing woman, who was going to be my role model, turned out to be one of the worst teachers I ever met in my life, who should have never been given the opportunity to shape young minds. She was mean, she was cold, she lacked any personal dimension, and she focused, from the very first lesson, only on the pack of wolves that were the chosen ones, who, unlike the rest of us mere mortals, had been doing advanced informatics in their primary school classes, while the rest of us were sat there drawing in Paint (the memories!!!). 

She was particularly mean and rude to me, and like any other sensitive 16-year-old with high hopes would react, I quickly came to dislike both her and her class. I never put effort into it, never wanted to do more than to pass, and interact as little as possible with her. Did she teach us anything? In reality, even with the chosen ones, she had a very unique approach, whereby she would ask them to memorise pieces of C++ code, not actually teaching them much either. 

So, you see, dear readers, I graduated high school deeply convinced my ‘coding’ times were behind me and I was never to code again in my life. But of course, life always has a way of surprising you. 

So here I am, world, 13 years after graduating, writing code daily, in my very computational PhD and additional projects. I am a bioinformatician, a data scientist, I write code to build my pipelines, I write code to analyse my datasets, and apparently, now I write code to build websites. And the truth is, dear reader, I absolutely love it! I wouldn’t change it for anything! I am very fortunate that I love my job and I chose a profession that truly and deeply spoke to my soul. 

And I can’t help wondering, dear reader, how much sooner I might have fallen in love with coding if signing up for informatics hadn’t turned out to be my very own “I volunteer as tribute!” moment.

Goodbye World!
